North Star · Our Mission

Beyond Institutions, There Is Home

Care For Taiwan returns care to people, to home, and to local culture. Through a cross-disciplinary team, an AI support system, and partnership with home medicine and Japan's Home Hospice network, we run a nationwide movement of Co-living Homes — so the final mile of acute illness, and the many lives institutions cannot hold, can be walked with dignity in their own villages and neighborhoods.

How We Practice · Co-living Homes Across Taiwan

When Neighbors
Become Family

A Co-living Home is not a type of long-term care institution. It is a home that local partners open — with their own resources, culture, and conditions — for lives that exist beyond institutions. Five homes are now in operation:

Taipei · Wenshan

Wenshan Co-living Home

Linli 123 Co-living Assoc. ╳ Fujen Charity Foundation

The "community living room" concept — bringing elders with disabilities, indigenous elders, and people with severe disabilities back into the neighborhood. House renovated in late 2024, opened three months later.

Taichung · Dongshi

Dongshi Co-living Home

Plahan Co-living Care Cooperative

11 residents · 2.5 days home each week — "a new kind of sojourn life, combining wisdom and care." Shortens the long medical-transport commute for tribal elders.

Nantou · Qingliu Tribe

Qingliu Tribe Co-living Home

Sderegan Cooperative (Lee Yu-Chin)

Seediq Tgdaya tribe, descendants of the Wushe Incident. The director opened her own home — turning railings, chairs, and towels into everyday rehab tools.

Taipei · Nangang

Nangang Co-living Home

Lourdes Association

A halfway house transformed into a co-living home. Serves people recovering from drug addiction and living with HIV — lives that institutions cannot fit, caught again by the community.

Taitung · Jhihben

Jhihben Co-living Home

Children's Bookhouse Foundation

Originally a youth service, now extending to elders. Partners with the local clinic for one-stop medical transport, long-term care, and family education.

New · Integrated Acute Care

What Institutions Cannot Hold,
We Hold at Home

Elders with acute or critical illness should not be "the last case sent to an institution." The Care For Taiwan family connects with home medicine teams to deliver three layers of relay — 24-hour home medicine, cross-disciplinary assessment, and Co-living Home residency — turning the "final mile" into the most familiar one.

  • 24h Home Medicine — integrated with the Home Medicine Society, Yunlin Haosuozhai, and Taitung Dulan Home Hospice. Doctors come to the home.
  • Cross-disciplinary Assessment — physical, occupational, nursing, and audiology in one visit. Everyday objects become rehab tools.
  • Co-living Home Reception — five sites (Wenshan, Dongshi, Qingliu, Nangang, Jhihben), each adapting to the lives and conditions in its own place.

Founder's Note
